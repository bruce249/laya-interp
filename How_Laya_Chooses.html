When Labels Override Evidence

Causal pathways for label shortcuts, option interaction and confidence in a non-autoregressive decision model

2026-10-09 · Vignesh Kanike · model: convaiinnovations/laya (laya 0.4.1)

Laya scores each answer option at its own [MASK] token in one encoder pass. Its yes/no answers follow the meaning of the option label words instead of the passage: swapping "yes"/"no" flips 96% of correct answers, while swapping "A"/"B" flips 3%. We trace this to a direct, redundant read of the label by the [MASK] in local attention layers, separate from an evidence route that runs through the question tokens. A one-edge guard removes the shortcut on untouched human-written data, and controlled retraining shows which kind of training data creates it. Order sensitivity and confidence are handled by separate, simpler mechanisms.

1 · Model and instrument

Laya is ModernBERT-large (28 layers, width 1024, 16 heads; global attention on every third layer from layer 0, a ±64-token window elsewhere) plus two transformer head layers and a per-option scorer. The input is [CLS] <type> question: <instructions> [SEP] [MASK] option0 [MASK] option1 … [SEP] state [SEP]. A noul (yes/no) option renders as "false: no, the statement does not hold" / "true: yes, the statement holds". All analysis uses an explicit re-implementation of the forward pass with named, editable sites, checked against laya's own predict.

Definitions

  • Paired transition. Each item is scored with normal and with swapped label words; we report counts of correct→correct, correct→wrong, wrong→correct and wrong→wrong.
  • Label-following = n(correct→wrong) / n(correct with normal labels); the denominator n is reported. Item-level 95% intervals are bootstrap intervals over those n items.
  • Swap gap = accuracy with normal labels − accuracy with swapped labels, over all items.
  • Recovery (patching / restoration) per item = (y_patched − y_corrupt) / (y_clean − y_corrupt), y = log-odds of the "holds" option; items with |y_clean − y_corrupt| < 0.5 are excluded and counted; we report the median with a bootstrap interval.
  • Seed-level tests use the trained model as the unit: per-seed values are listed and compared with an exact two-sided permutation test (and Welch's t-test).
  • Confirmatory vs exploratory. The frozen benchmark (600 MNLI + QNLI items, SHA-256 cea5547f…) and its interventions were fixed before evaluation; analyses added afterwards are labelled exploratory.

2 · C3: labels override evidence

2.1 Behaviour

Test: each option's label word is swapped ("true: no, the statement does not hold"). A model reading the passage is unaffected; a model reading the label word picks the option the passage contradicts. Label-following is the share of items answered correctly with normal labels that flip when the words are swapped. The effect replicates on 300 held-out BoolQ items and on GPT-written items, and laya's model card documents the failure; our contribution is the mechanism and fix.

  • Label read is redundant and mostly late. Blocking [MASK]→label in layers 10–14 alone leaves label-following at 96%; blocking layers 21–27 alone halves it (53%); blocking it everywhere except any single band from 0–20 removes it. An earlier account placing the read at layers 10–14 was wrong and is withdrawn.
  • Evidence goes through the question tokens. Stopping them from reading the passage drops accuracy from 0.737 to 0.537; stopping the [MASK]s or option text from reading it changes nothing. Restoring only the question tokens at layer 16 recovers 0.91 of the decision.
  • No timing race. Label information leaves the label tokens around layer 20 and enters the [MASK] at layers 21–23, the same layers where the evidence arrives.
  • Contradicting passages (187 BoolQ passages edited by gpt-5.6-terra so the answer flips, checked by gpt-5.6-luna): with consistent labels laya follows the edited passage 71% of the time, with swapped labels 32%. With the passage blanked, swapping labels moves "holds" answers from 22% to 91%.

2.3 The guard

The guard blocks noul [MASK]s from attending to their label words in local layers only, installed on a stock laya.load agent and run through the official predict API (interp/label_guard.py). On BoolQ yes/no it cuts label-following from 95.9% to 1.3%. Choice questions are bit-identical (max difference 0); jailbreak AUROC moves 0.940 → 0.919 (1.8% of decisions change); injection AUROC 0.898 → 0.870.

Fifteen models were trained from ModernBERT-large on the same 31,419 items with laya's loss; only the yes/no labels differed. Only training where the label word is the sole cue to which option means what (shuffled order, identical option text) creates the shortcut (p = 0.000 vs baseline, seeds as units); meaningful labels alone do not (p = 0.46). The guard's effect on the planted models differs by seed (28%, 80%, 13%, 59%, 27%, 77%): the same behaviour is implemented by different routes, so a fix derived from one model's internals can fail on a retrained sibling. Laya's own training data are not public, so this shows what can create the shortcut, not what did.

3 · C1: order sensitivity is positional; competition is separate

Reversing the options flips 29.6% of laya's multiple-choice answers (fp32, 1,392 items). Options do compete: blanking one shifts another's centred logit by 0.37 on average. Slot index explains 0.2% of order-induced variance, so this is not a simple primacy bias.

Position binds each [MASK] to its option. Sharing positions while leaving attention unrestricted makes every [MASK] identical (same token, position and keys), so all options score the same (accuracy 0.246). The factorial therefore holds binding fixed (each [MASK] reads only its own option) and varies option-text interaction × positions. With shared positions and symmetric masks the model is order-invariant by construction; the measured quantities are the accuracy cost and how much competition survives.

Position permutation (Phase 8): giving the options reversed position ids without moving the tokens reproduces the reversed run in 100% of flips in all 10 models. This holds by construction, since a bidirectional encoder sees only (token, position) pairs, so order effects are positional by definition; the substantive result is the factorial one above. The competition measure is robust to the replacement used (laya: filler 0.39, dash 0.40, distractor 0.44).

A competitor's content builds up in the target option's text tokens through about layer 16, then enters the target [MASK] at layers 17–22; no small set of heads carries it.

4 · C2: a late-MLP logit amplifier

Removing MLPs 22, 27, 26 halves laya's logit scale (ratio 0.47) and fixes most miscalibration, but per-item rescaling reproduces it and a single temperature beats it, so by the pre-registered rule these MLPs are reported as an amplifier, not a confidence regulator. The effect sits entirely at the [MASK] positions. Unlike the entropy neurons described for decoder LLMs, which the authors distinguish from a temperature, these behave like one.

5 · C4: jailbreak and injection (supporting)

  • Detection AUROC 0.947 on natural jailbreaks, 0.867 on injections.
  • A linear probe reads the attack from layer 0 at 0.92; patching shows the decision carried by the prompt until layer 15, relayed by question tokens, and completed in the head layers. This parallels the early-decodable, late-decisive pattern Wen et al. (2026) report for prompt-injection compliance in decoder LLMs. Unlike their late layer, which tracks intent and resists obfuscation, laya's decision is sensitive to surface style (next items).
  • Adding the jailbreak direction at the layer-20 [MASK]s raises benign prompts flagged from 23% to 61% with an unrelated question at 3%.
  • Override removal (exploratory, revised after audit): gpt-5.6-terra was asked to remove only the override from 145 flagged jailbreaks, and laya still flagged 82% of the edits. An audit of 60 random edits found that only 21 removed just the override; most also cut the persona and setup, and 7 kept the override. On the 21 clean edits laya still flags 17 (81%, 95% CI 60–92%). What remains in them is role-play framing or a harmful request, so laya responds to these rather than to the override itself; with 21 items this is supporting evidence only, and the style test below is the cleaner test.
  • Style test: the same 150 harmless requests are flagged 12.7% written plainly and 54.0% written in jailbreak style (persona/role-play, no override); for the injection question 2.7% vs 42.7%. The pairs are GPT-written; the audit found 141/150 make the same request (the rest add "show your steps") and none contains an override.
  • Hindi (laya-multilingual): projecting out the English-fit direction drops detection of translated jailbreaks from 52% to 8.5%.

7 · Related work

Order and option-ID bias are documented for decoder LLMs (Pezeshkpour & Hruschka 2024; Zheng et al. 2024), and Anchored Answers traces GPT-2's preference for "A" to MLP value vectors. Set-Based Prompting removes order dependence by sharing positions and masking attention; its ablation on 20 MMLU questions found the mask more important than positions in decoders, the opposite of what blocking attention alone does in laya. Label Words are Anchors studies in-context demonstration labels in decoders, a different setting from answer-option labels. Confidence Regulation Neurons describes entropy neurons that are explicitly not a temperature; laya's late MLPs are. Wen et al. (2026) report early-decodable, late-decisive prompt-injection compliance in five decoder LLMs, with a late representation that tracks intent; laya shares the timing, but not the robustness to surface style. Different seeds learning different circuits is known (A Toy Model of Universality); our retrained seeds share the edge class but differ in layers. A BERT cross-encoder BM25 circuit is the closest encoder precedent found. Full notes and the search log are in LITERATURE_NOTES.md.

8 · Limitations and corrections

  • Laya was not built for multiple-choice QA (its model card notes near-chance zero-shot performance on typed decisions); C1 and C2 describe how it handles options, not tuned QA behaviour.
  • Order-invariance is guaranteed by construction in the shared-position cells; positions alone could not be tested.
  • The extended guard was designed on earlier sets and has passed one confirmatory test (the frozen benchmark); contradicting passages and jailbreak rewrites were checked by a second GPT model and a Claude audit (next item), not yet by hand; SAE feature labels were contaminated and are not used.
  • Audit of GPT-made data (random samples, judged by Claude, a model independent of the GPT generators; human review of the flagged rows is pending): contradicting edits on the frozen benchmark flip the answer in 56/60 (93%, 95% CI 84–97%); BoolQ edits 57/60 (95%); GPT-written C3 items have a correct gold label in 60/60 and are fully determined by the passage in 57/60; style pairs make the same request in 141/150 (the 9 others add "show your steps") and none contains an override; jailbreak rewrites removed the override in 53/60 but removed only the override in 21/60 (35%, 95% CI 24–48%).
  • The all-layer guard's pre-registered prediction failed on retrained models (3 of 6 seeds ≤ 10%); RoBERTa has 3 seeds per arm, so its comparison cannot reach p < 0.10.
  • Withdrawn or corrected during the study: the early-timing account of label reading; "a confidence regulator" (now amplifier); a single-seed "20% less competition" (now a non-significant trend); "steering works only late"; "label-following comes from training data" (now: which training can create it); "same behaviour, different mechanisms" (now: the same edge classes in different layers, partly entangled with normal answering in some seeds); "a redundant label is not used" (wrong: 4 of 6 seeds use it); "laya flags 82% after only the override is removed" (the edits removed more than the override; 81% on 21 audited clean edits, exploratory); the planned "position vs token order" test (an identity by construction, not evidence).
